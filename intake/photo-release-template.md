# Photo Release — Draft Template

> **This is a starting draft, not legal advice.** Have an attorney review it before use, especially
> since requirements vary by state. It's here so the ask isn't blocked on drafting one from scratch.

**Why this exists:** performing work on someone's home does not grant permission to publish photos of
it. Under US copyright law Tino owns the photos he takes, but that's separate from the homeowner's
interest in their own property and privacy. A signed release closes the gap. Background in
[`../docs/01-legal-and-licensing.md`](../docs/01-legal-and-licensing.md#6-photos-privacy-and-prior-employer-work).

**When to ask:** at project closeout, while the client is happy with the work. Pair it with the review
request — one conversation, two asks. Coming back a year later is much harder.

---

## Draft — English

**PHOTO AND PROJECT RELEASE**

Project address: ______________________________________________

Client name: _________________________________________________

Date of completion: ___________________________________________

I give [BUSINESS NAME] permission to photograph and video the work performed at the property above,
and to use those images and recordings to show examples of its work. This includes use on its
website, social media accounts, printed materials, project proposals, and bid submissions.

I understand and agree that:

- [BUSINESS NAME] will **not** publish my name, street address, house number, or contact information
  alongside these images. The project may be identified by city or general area only.
- The images will show the work performed and the property, and will not include identifiable images
  of me or my family unless I initial here: ______
- I will not receive payment for this use.
- I may withdraw this permission for future use at any time by writing to [BUSINESS EMAIL].
  [BUSINESS NAME] will remove the images from materials it controls within a reasonable period, but
  cannot recall materials already printed or distributed.
- [BUSINESS NAME] owns the photographs it takes.

Client signature: _____________________________ Date: ____________

Printed name: _________________________________________________

Contact (not for publication): ________________________________

## Notes on using this

- **Keep the signed copies.** Scan them and note `releaseOnFile: true` on the project entry, per the
  [project template](project-template.md#photos--permission-gates).
- **A signed release does not cover prior-employer work.** That needs clearance from the employer, not
  the homeowner — a different problem with a different party.
- **Consider a digital version.** A simple form he can send by text at closeout will get signed far
  more often than a piece of paper he has to remember to bring.
- The withdrawal clause is deliberate. It makes the release easier to sign and costs almost nothing in
  practice.
