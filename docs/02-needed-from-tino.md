# Everything We Need From Tino

The master list, ordered by what blocks work rather than by category. A plain-language version to
actually hand him is at [`intake/questionnaire-en.md`](../intake/questionnaire-en.md) (Spanish:
[`questionnaire-es.md`](../intake/questionnaire-es.md)). Track arrival in
[`intake/asset-tracker.csv`](../intake/asset-tracker.csv).

Tier 1 blocks design and copy. Tier 2 blocks launch. Tier 3 unlocks the commercial track. Tier 4
makes it better over time.

---

## Tier 1 — Blockers (we cannot write the service list or lay out a page without these)

| # | Item | Why it blocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **State and city of operation** | Almost every legal and SEO decision turns on this. Advertising rules, license display placement, trade-licensing requirements, service-area strategy. |
| 2 | **Exact license type, classification codes, number, issuing board, expiration date** + a scan | Goes in the header on every page. Several states legally require it in all advertising. Classification codes tell us what we may advertise. |
| 3 | **Which trades is he actually licensed for?** Specifically electrical and plumbing — licensed, or subcontracted? | Advertising an unlicensed trade is a misdemeanor in CA and TX. This determines the entire service list. See [01-legal-and-licensing.md](01-legal-and-licensing.md#1-blocker-we-may-not-be-able-to-advertise-electrical-or-plumbing). |
| 4 | **What "certified" means** — which credential, from whom | It's in the brief but it's a regulated term of art in some states. |
| 5 | **Business name decision** — the LLC name and the domain, and whether they match | Everything downstream (logo, email, Google profile, footer, license advertising) depends on it. He reportedly has an idea already — we need it confirmed and checked for availability. |
| 6 | **LLC status** — formed? EIN? Does the entity name match the license exactly? | Name mismatches break local SEO and violate CA advertising rules. |
| 7 | **Insurance** — GL limits, workers' comp status, commercial auto, agent contact | We can't print "insured" otherwise, and GCs won't prequalify him without it. |
| 8 | **How he wants to be contacted, honestly** | This determines the whole call-to-action architecture. See the sub-questions below — this is the most underrated item on the list. |

### On #8, specifically

Speed of response is plausibly the highest-leverage variable in the whole project (see
[06-leadgen-and-local-seo.md](06-leadgen-and-local-seo.md#speed-to-lead)). So we need real answers,
not aspirational ones:

- Will he actually answer a call within five minutes during the workday? While on a roof?
- Does he text? Is texting preferred? (Many trades customers now prefer it.)
- One phone number or two (business vs. personal)?
- WhatsApp? It is near-universal in Spanish-language contractor marketing and nearly absent from
  mainstream US contractor sites — a cheap edge.
- Who covers evenings and weekends? Nobody is a valid answer, but then we shouldn't promise a
  response time.
- Does he want a form at all, or is the form just a fallback for people who won't call?

If the answer is "he's on a jobsite and can't pick up," the site should promise a callback window it
can keep and send an instant auto-text on form submit. Overpromising here is worse than saying
nothing.

## Tier 2 — Needed for launch

### Business identity

- [ ] Business phone he answers, and whether it can receive texts
- [ ] Business email (we'll want one at the domain, not a personal Gmail)
- [ ] Physical address, **or** a service-area radius if he works from home — and an explicit
      decision about whether the address is published. Home-based businesses can run a
      service-area-only Google profile.
- [ ] Service radius in miles, plus the named cities and neighborhoods he'll actually drive to
- [ ] Business hours, and separately, emergency availability
- [ ] Any existing online presence to claim rather than duplicate: Google Business Profile,
      Facebook, Instagram, Angi, Yelp, Thumbtack, Houzz, Nextdoor
- [ ] Logo, if one exists. If not, we need to plan for one.
- [ ] Preferred payment methods (cards? Zelle? checks? financing?) — small sites that list these
      convert better than you'd expect

### The revenue question (drives SEO and the whole homepage)

- [ ] **Which two or three services does he most want to be hired for?** Not everything he *can* do —
      what he wants the phone to ring about. This sets the Google Business Profile primary category,
      which is the single strongest local-ranking lever we control. The common mistake is
      aspirational: choosing "General Contractor" when the money is in kitchen remodels.
- [ ] Typical job size he wants (is a $400 drywall patch welcome, or a distraction?)
- [ ] Jobs he does *not* want
- [ ] Rough price ranges per service type — see the pricing section below

### Projects

For each project, we need a filled-in copy of
[`intake/project-template.md`](../intake/project-template.md). **Target 8–12 projects for launch**;
more is better but 8 good ones beat 30 thin ones.

The four we already know about should be first, because they're his own property and therefore
unencumbered by client permission or NDA:

1. **The house addition** — doubled the square footage himself. This is the flagship. We want the
   before/after, the square footage numbers, the timeline, what trades it involved, whether it was
   permitted and inspected.
2. **The 4-car carport** — span, materials, foundation, timeline.
3. **The outdoor kitchen / covered patio** — roof, sink, stove. This means he ran plumbing, gas or
   electrical, and structural work in one project. Great story.
4. **The multiple house renovations** — ovens, stoves, tile, paint, lighting. Good "before/after"
   material and good evidence of finish quality.

Per project we need: what it was, scope, approximate value or a range, city, year, duration, what
trades it involved, any interesting problem he solved, and whether the client will act as a
reference.

The Houzz data is specific about why this matters: homeowners rank *experience with a similar
project scope* at 47% and *similar style* at only 16%. **So organize the portfolio by type and size
of job, not by aesthetic.**

### Photos

- [ ] **20–40 real project photos minimum.** No stock photos, ever — over half of contractor sites
      use them and it reads instantly as fake.
- [ ] Before/after pairs shot from the *same position* wherever possible. This unlocks comparison
      sliders, which almost no small contractor has.
- [ ] Photos of Tino working — face visible, on a real jobsite. Only about 4 of 21 sites we reviewed
      have real team photos; it's the most-skipped high-trust element.
- [ ] One decent portrait for the About page
- [ ] Detail shots (tile edges, trim, panel work) — these prove finish quality better than wide shots
- [ ] For **each** photo: who took it, whose property it is, and whether it was done for a previous
      employer. This is a legal gate, not paperwork for its own sake — see
      [01-legal-and-licensing.md](01-legal-and-licensing.md#4-photos-the-prior-employer-problem-is-the-sharp-edge).
- [ ] Signed photo releases for any client property
- [ ] Video, if he has any. Even phone walkthroughs. Almost nobody at his scale does video.

Shooting guidance to send him: [`intake/photo-guide.md`](../intake/photo-guide.md).

### Words

- [ ] **His story, in his own words.** How he got into the trades, how long, why he's going
      independent, what he cares about in the work. Voice memo is fine — better than fine, actually,
      because we can transcribe it and keep his real phrasing. Sites that argue a point of view
      outperform sites that list adjectives.
- [ ] Years of experience, total
- [ ] Any existing testimonials, reviews, or references — with permission to quote and attribute
- [ ] Does he offer a warranty? What term, covering what? (7 of 21 sites state a term; it's a
      differentiator.)
- [ ] How he estimates and communicates during a job. Houzz found the top homeowner complaints are
      trust (48%), unclear proposals (34%), and poor communication (30%) — so a page explaining his
      process addresses the top three objections directly. A sample redacted estimate would be even
      stronger.
- [ ] Does he pull permits? Which jurisdictions? (Strong trust signal against unlicensed competitors.)
- [ ] **Spanish:** does he want a bilingual site? Does he prefer to be contacted in Spanish? Should
      there be a Spanish phone line?

### Pricing (optional, but the single biggest differentiator we found)

Only 2 of 21 sites we reviewed publish price ranges. The one that does it best publishes three tiers
per service, each with a dollar range, a timeline, and a scope list. It pre-qualifies leads so he
stops driving to estimates he can't win, and it captures the "how much does X cost" searches that
competitors cede to national aggregators.

Ask him for, per main service, three tiers — budget / mid / high — with a range, a rough timeline,
and what's included. If he's uncomfortable publishing numbers, a "starting at" or a typical range is
still better than silence. This is a conversation, not a form field.

## Tier 3 — The commercial / municipal track

This is where his rarest asset lives. Detail in
[07-commercial-bid-playbook.md](07-commercial-bid-playbook.md).

- [ ] **Full résumé of the pipe-network experience.** Employers, years, project types, pipe
      diameters and materials, depths, agencies and municipalities, project values, his actual role
      (design? layout? foreman? superintendent?). This is the backbone of the capability statement
      and it's all prose, so no photo-permission problem.
- [ ] Whether he did *design* work on those networks or only construction — "designing" pipe
      networks is a much stronger claim and we need to know exactly what it means
- [ ] Safety and technical certifications, with cards: OSHA 10/30, competent person for
      trenching/excavation, confined space, traffic control/flagger, NASSCO PACP/MACP/LACP, welding,
      backflow, erosion control
- [ ] Equipment he owns or has access to (excavators, trailers, compactors, laser levels, pipe
      lasers). Civil contractors sell equipment lists as proof of capability.
- [ ] What he self-performs vs. subcontracts — publishable as a percentage, which reads as very
      credible on the civil side
- [ ] Bonding: any surety relationship? A capacity letter?
- [ ] Financial readiness: bookkeeping system, CPA relationship, whether statements exist. Needed
      for prequalification above roughly $250K.
- [ ] References from the commercial side — former supervisors, agency contacts, GCs
- [ ] Ownership documentation (51%+) and whether he wants to pursue MBE certification. **Note:** the
      federal landscape here changed materially in 2023–2026 and being Hispanic no longer carries an
      automatic presumption — read the playbook before promising anything.
- [ ] Written clearance from former employers for any project photos, or confirmation we're
      describing it in prose only

## Tier 4 — Ongoing / nice to have

- [ ] A steady drip of new project photos (a habit, not a one-time dump — Google rewards recency)
- [ ] Reviews from each completed job, on Google first, then a second platform. 74% of consumers
      check at least two review sites.
- [ ] Owner responses to reviews — 89% of consumers expect them, on positive reviews too
- [ ] Short jobsite videos
- [ ] Any awards, manufacturer certifications, supplier relationships
- [ ] Answers to the questions customers keep asking him, verbatim — free FAQ content

## The five things to ask for first

If we only get one conversation, ask these:

1. **What state, and what exactly does your license cover?** (Unblocks everything.)
2. **Are you licensed for electrical and plumbing, or do you sub them out?** (Legal exposure.)
3. **What's the business name going to be?** (Unblocks domain, logo, email, profile.)
4. **Photos of your house addition, the carport, and the outdoor kitchen — before and after.**
   (Unblocks the portfolio, and it's the material with no permission problem.)
5. **When someone calls you, how fast can you really pick up?** (Unblocks the CTA design.)
