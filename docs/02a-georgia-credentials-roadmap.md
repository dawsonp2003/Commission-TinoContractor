# Georgia Credentials Roadmap

The most useful thing the Georgia research turned up: **his municipal pipe experience maps onto a real
Georgia license that very few multi-trade contractors hold**, and the path to it is achievable on
experience rather than schooling.

This doc is the licensing plan. Legal constraints are in
[01-legal-and-licensing.md](01-legal-and-licensing.md); commercial bid access is in
[07-commercial-bid-playbook.md](07-commercial-bid-playbook.md).

## The recommended order

Cheapest and fastest first, because each one starts producing value immediately:

| # | Credential | Requirement highlights | Exam? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Level 1A Erosion & Sediment Control** ("blue card") | ~8-hour Fundamentals Seminar | Yes |
| 2 | **12-hour utility safety training** | Trenching/excavation competent person + confined space | No |
| 3 | **Utility Foreman certificate** | 1 year field experience ≥5 ft deep + #2 + notarized letters | **No** |
| 4 | **Utility Manager certificate** | 2 yr foreman (or equivalent out-of-state) + 3 yr total + #2 | Yes |
| 5 | **Utility Contractor license** (the LLC) | Written safety policy + full-time Utility Manager on staff | No |
| 6 | **Chapter 41 contractor license** | 2–4 years experience, insurance, net worth, exam | Yes |

Steps 1–3 are quick, cheap, and immediately marketable. Steps 4–5 unlock the specialty. Step 6 unlocks
the residential remodel business and is the one with the longest lead time, so it should start in
parallel.

**Nothing about the underground specialty can be advertised until step 5 is complete** — see
[01-legal-and-licensing.md §3](01-legal-and-licensing.md#3-the-utility-contractor-license-his-biggest-opportunity-and-a-live-constraint).

## Do this first, today: the notarized experience letters

**Rules 121-2-.08(3) and 121-2-.09(3)** require **notarized letters on company letterhead from each
employer listed** on the Part II Experience Record, stating dates, role, and the nature of the utility
work.

This is the single most time-sensitive item in the entire project. HR departments reorganize,
supervisors retire, companies get acquired, and records get purged. Every month of delay makes these
harder to obtain, and **without them the Utility Manager application cannot be filed at all.**

He should request them now, before anything else on this list — and before the website exists, since
asking a former employer for a favor is easier before you're visibly competing with them.

What each letter needs:

- Company letterhead, notarized
- Dates of employment
- His role and title
- The nature of the utility work: sanitary sewer, storm drainage, water mains
- **Explicit confirmation the work was on systems at least five feet underground** — this is the
  statutory trigger and the reviewer will look for it

## 1. Level 1A Erosion & Sediment Control — start here

**O.C.G.A. § 12-7-19**, administered by the Georgia Soil and Water Conservation Commission, rules at
Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. Chapter 600-8-1.

§ 12-7-19(b)(1): "**Persons involved in land development design, review, permitting, construction,
monitoring, or inspection or any land-disturbing activity shall meet the education and training
certification requirements.**"

The **Level 1A Fundamentals Seminar** is explicitly aimed at "builders, contractors, developers, site
superintendents, **grading and utility contractors**, and monitoring consultants." Certification
requires attending an approved seminar and passing the exam (Rule 600-8-1-.04(2)(a)). Local
governments describe it as an 8-hour course, though the rules don't fix an hour count.

**Why first:** trenching for sewer, storm drain, and water mains is land-disturbing activity. Any
project needing an NPDES construction general permit needs certified personnel on site, and GCs and
public owners routinely require the blue card of underground crews and superintendents. It's the
cheapest, fastest, most immediately marketable credential on the list.

Certification expires **no later than three years** after issuance, with 4 hours of CE for renewal
(§ 12-7-19(f)(1)). Note that § 12-7-19 incorporates the § 50-36-1 lawful-presence requirement — a
signed sworn affidavit plus at least one secure and verifiable document.

**Level II Introduction to Design is not available to him.** It's restricted to registered design
professionals — engineers, architects, landscape architects, land surveyors, or CPESC holders. He'd
need to retain a Level II Certified Design Professional to prepare or seal an ES&PC plan. Worth knowing
before we describe him as doing design work.

## 2. The 12-hour utility safety training

**Rule 121-2-.10(1)** requires "at least **12 hours of actual instruction** including **Competent
Person in Trenching and Excavation** and **Confined Space Entry** Training," covering protective-system
design, initial inspections, soil assessment, checking for fissures, load-resistance capacity,
stability of adjoining structures, and daily/changed-condition inspections, per 29 C.F.R. 1926
Subpart P.

Must be a board-approved course. It's a prerequisite for both the Foreman and Manager certificates, so
it's not optional. Utility Manager requires **safety-training recertification every two years**
(§ 43-14-8.3(b)).

Worth noting: OSHA issues no "competent person" card. The deliverable is training plus, once he's an
employer, a written designation letter.

## 3. Utility Foreman — the quick win, no exam

**O.C.G.A. § 43-14-8.4**, Rule 121-2-.09. Requires:

- **1 year** of field experience on utility systems ≥5 ft underground
- The 12-hour safety course
- Notarized employer letters
- **No exam**

**Why this is worth doing on its own:** § 43-14-8.4(c) requires a certified utility manager *or
foreman* **present at the job site** whenever a utility system is being built or repaired. Holding the
foreman certificate alone makes him immediately employable by licensed utility contractors — real
income while he assembles the Manager package, and it builds the Georgia-specific track record the
Manager application wants.

---

## 4. Utility Manager — the key credential

**O.C.G.A. § 43-14-8.3**, Rule 121-2-.08. Requires:

- **2 years as a licensed utility foreman in Georgia, OR equivalent out-of-state experience as a
  manager or foreman in the field** ← the clause that makes this achievable for him
- **3 years total** experience in construction, erection, alteration, or repair of utility systems at
  least five feet underground
- The 12-hour safety course
- Notarized letters on letterhead from each employer on the Experience Record
- Pass the exam at 70. **Oral administration available on request** (§ 43-14-8.3(c)) — worth knowing if
  written exams aren't his strength or if he'd prefer to test in a different format.
- Recertification safety training every 2 years

**Reciprocity** is available for out-of-state utility manager licensees under Rule 121-2-.03(2), if he
ever held one.

Years at major construction companies on municipal sewer, storm drainage, and water mains should
comfortably satisfy both the 3-year total and the 2-year manager/foreman component via the
out-of-state-equivalent path. **The binding constraint is documentation, not experience** — hence the
letters.

## 5. Utility Contractor license — held by the LLC

**O.C.G.A. § 43-14-8.2**, Rule 121-2-.07. The business holds this one. Requires:

- Application listing owners and officers
- A **written safety policy** meeting board minimums (Rule 121-2-.07(1)(b)): concern and support of
  safety, continuing efforts to provide a safe environment, compliance with state and federal safety
  requirements, maintenance of a safety program, and regularly scheduled safety meetings for all field
  personnel
- Attestation that **at least one certified Utility Manager is employed full-time at each permanent
  office** — i.e. him
- Fees

**Two operational constraints worth designing around now:**

- **Rule 121-6-.01(1): he may qualify only ONE business at a time.** So the LLC structure needs to be
  right before he applies.
- Each additional permanent office needs its own resident Utility Manager (Rule 121-6-.03). Severance
  must be reported within 5 days; § 43-14-8.2(f) grants a 90-day grace period (180 max per two-year
  period) to replace a departed Manager before revocation.

**One unresolved ambiguity:** § 43-14-8.2 refers to Class A, Class B, and Class U utility contractor
licenses, and § 43-14-6(a)(4)(H) makes "bidding in excess of license coverage" a disciplinary ground —
implying dollar tiers. **But no definition of those classes appears anywhere in Chapter 14 or the
Department 121 rules**, and Rule 121-2-.07 describes a single undifferentiated statewide license. This
is a real gap between statute and published rules. **Call the division before relying on any class
distinction.**

## 6. Chapter 41 contractor license — for the residential side

Administered by the **State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors** (Secretary of
State, Professional Licensing Boards Division) under O.C.G.A. Title 43 Ch. 41, rules at Department 553.

| License | Scope | Net worth | Min. general liability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Residential-Basic** | Detached 1–2 family residences, 1-family townhouses ≤3 stories, accessory buildings — § 43-41-2(10) | Financial responsibility per application | **$300,000** (R. 553-3-.01(4)) |
| **Residential-Light Commercial** | The above plus multifamily and mixed-use light commercial <4 stories, <25,000 sq ft, or pre-engineered steel ≤50,000 sq ft — § 43-41-2(11) | Same | **$500,000** (R. 553-3-.03(4)) |
| **General Contractor (Limited Tier)** | Any construction work, **no single contract over $1,000,000** (R. 553-4-.05(8)) | **$25,000** | **$500,000** |
| **General Contractor** | Any construction work, no cap | **$150,000** | **$500,000** |

**Experience paths** (Rules 553-3-.01, 553-3-.03, 553-4-.01, 553-4-.05) — must be 21+:

- **Residential-Basic:** 2 years working as or for a residential contractor, predominantly
  residential-basic
- **Light Commercial and both GC tiers:** either (i) a four-year degree in engineering, architecture,
  construction management or building construction plus 1 year experience; (ii) accredited college
  credit plus practical experience totaling 4 years; or (iii) **4 years of active construction-industry
  experience, at least 2 as or for a general contractor** — and for the GC tiers, **at least 1 year in
  administration, marketing, accounting, estimating, drafting, engineering, supervision, or project
  management**

> **That last clause matters.** Pure field labor doesn't satisfy the GC path. His superintendent and
> project-management history should qualify — but he needs to **document it in those terms**, not as
> field experience. Worth framing the experience letters accordingly.

All four require a board exam (plans and specs, codes, estimating, ethics, contracting, workers' comp,
insurance, surety bonding, liens — R. 553-4-.01(5)(b)), proof of workers' compensation, and tax
verification. Insurance certificates stay on file and are a **condition of renewal**; any coverage
change must be reported within 30 days.

**Renewal is biennial, due June 30 of even-numbered years**, with a penalty window through Dec 31 —
after which failure to renew "shall have the same effect as a revocation" (R. 553-12-.05(3)).
Continuing education: **3 hours/year** Residential-Basic, **6 hours/year** Light Commercial
(R. 553-12-.02(2)(a)). The GC rules contain no CE mandate.

### Which one?

Depends on his answer about target work:

- **Residential-Basic** if the near-term focus is houses, additions, and remodels. Lowest bar, lowest
  insurance minimum. Note it's limited by *structure type*, not dollars.
- **General Contractor Limited Tier** if he wants commercial and municipal building work too. $25,000
  net worth is a modest hurdle and the $1M per-contract cap is far above anything he'd bid initially.

**Buy $500,000 of general liability regardless** — it satisfies every classification, so it removes the
need to re-shop insurance if he upgrades later.

*Fees, unverified:* secondary sources consistently report **$200 application, $100 biennial renewal,
$200 late fee** for Chapter 41, and roughly **$30–50 application** with **$199–256 exam** and **$75
biennial renewal** for Chapter 14. Confirm with the boards.

## What the site can say, and when

This is the practical output. Each row is gated on a credential.

| Site content | Requires | Available now? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repair work, non-structural | § 43-41-17(g) — **with** written disclosure that he holds no Chapter 41 license | Yes |
| Drywall, tile, paint, framing, carpentry (over $2,500) | Chapter 41 license | **No — gated** |
| Additions, remodels, carports, outdoor structures | Chapter 41 license | **No — gated** |
| On-site stormwater that connects to no public/private system | Nothing extra (§ 43-41-2(4)) | Yes |
| Sewer / storm drain / water main services | Utility Contractor license | **No — gated** |
| **Pipe-network experience described in role terms** | Nothing — it's biography, not an offer | **Yes** |
| Electrical, plumbing, HVAC as services | Chapter 14 trade license (he won't have these) | **Never — subcontract framing only** |
| "Level 1A Erosion & Sediment Control Certified — GSWCC" | That certification | After step 1 |
| Erosion/sediment control **plan design** | Level II — requires PE/RA/RLA/LS/CPESC | **Not available to him** |

**The sequencing insight:** the commercial page can go live before the utility license, because
describing his experience and role is biography rather than an offer to perform. The residential
service pages cannot go live before the Chapter 41 license, because
[offering *is* contracting under § 43-41-2(3)](01-legal-and-licensing.md#4-certified-and-licensed-are-the-two-riskiest-words-on-the-site).
