# Commercial & Municipal Playbook

Tino's municipal pipe-network experience — designing and building sanitary sewer, storm drainage, and
water mains at major construction companies — is the rarest thing about him and the hardest for a
homeowner-focused competitor to fake. It's also the side of the business with the highest ticket
sizes and the least price shopping.

This doc covers what a company or agency needs to see before hiring him, and what that means for the
site.

---

## The capability statement

A one-page PDF, and **nobody in our 21-site sample hosted one.** A GC's estimator who can download it
at 9pm shortlists Tino over someone who has to be emailed and waited on. This is the single highest-
leverage commercial asset and it's a document, not a feature.

The SBA publishes the canonical structure in its
[capabilities statement guide](https://ussba.github.io/brand/assets/sba/resource-partners/8a-CapabilitesStatement-508.pdf)
— five elements, one page:

1. **Core competencies** — 4–6 specific services, worded the way agencies describe the work
2. **Differentiators** — bonding capacity, EMR, equipment owned, response time, self-performance
3. **Past performance** — recent relevant projects with client, value, and outcome
4. **Corporate data** — UEI, CAGE code, NAICS codes, licenses, certifications
5. **Contact information**

Relevant NAICS codes for Tino:

| Code | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **237110** | Water and sewer line and related structures construction — *his specialty* |
| **236220** | Commercial and institutional building construction ($45M small-business size standard) |
| **238210** | Electrical contractors |
| **238110** | Poured concrete foundation and structure contractors |

UEI replaced DUNS in April 2022. Both UEI and CAGE come free with SAM.gov registration.

**Site implication:** a `/commercial` page that ends in a download button, plus the same facts laid
out as HTML on the page so they're indexable and readable on a phone.

## What GCs require to prequalify a subcontractor

[DPR Construction](https://www.dpr.com/subcontractor-prequalification),
[McGough](https://www.mcgough.com/prequalifications/),
[Rudolph & Sletten](https://www.rsconstruction.com/subcontractors/), and
[BCCI](https://bcciconst.com/for-subcontractors/) publish their requirements openly. Read them as the
spec for what Tino must be able to produce:

- Signed **W-9**
- **Certificate of Insurance** — $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate is the common floor, $2M/$4M on
  higher-risk work; commercial auto $1M CSL; workers' comp at statutory limits; additional-insured
  endorsement on a primary, non-contributory basis
- **EMR verification letter** from the carrier, typically covering three years (1.0 is the industry
  average; acceptance thresholds usually 1.0–1.25; BCCI flags anything above 1.2 as requiring a
  remediation plan)
- **Surety letter** stating single-project, aggregate, and available bonding capacity (DPR wants it
  dated within 30 days)
- **CPA-prepared financial statements**, plus interim balance sheet and P&L
- Licenses, three project references, and any MBE/DBE/WBE certificates
- Three years of **OSHA 300/300A logs**

### The realistic entry point

Two facts make this less daunting than the list suggests:

- **McGough approves subcontractors up to $250,000 by default with no financials submitted.** Small
  commercial work is the way in.
- A brand-new LLC **has no EMR** — there's no loss history to rate. Expect to explain this rather
  than hide it. Tino's personal safety record and certifications at prior employers are the
  substitute, which is another reason to collect the certification cards.

Most GCs will sign an NDA before receiving financials, so his reluctance to hand those over is
normal and negotiable.

## Minority certifications — read this before promising anything

**The legal ground shifted and most advice online is now wrong.**

- In *Ultima Services Corp. v. USDA*, 683 F. Supp. 3d 745 (E.D. Tenn., July 19, 2023), a federal
  court held the SBA's **rebuttable presumption of social disadvantage** for designated racial groups
  unconstitutional and enjoined its use. SBA stopped applying it program-wide.
- SBA issued a **final rule on August 11, 2026, effective September 10, 2026**, formally removing the
  presumption for individually-owned firms and replacing the narrative approach with a two-part test:
  evidence that a government or private entity's policy or practice disadvantaged the applicant's
  group, plus self-certification of resulting material harm
  ([Davis Wright Tremaine analysis](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/government-contracts-insider/2026/08/sba-8a-social-disadvantage-rule)).
- **DBE followed:** a USDOT Interim Final Rule dated October 3, 2025 removed race- and sex-based
  presumptions, after the 2024 preliminary injunction in *Mid-America Milling v. USDOT*.

> **We cannot tell Tino that being Hispanic qualifies him for federal set-asides.** He would need to
> build an evidentiary case under the new framework, and the rules are actively in flux. Anyone
> telling him otherwise is working from pre-2023 information.

What still works:

| Program | Issuer | Cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **NMSDC MBE** | Regional council | ~$350–650/yr | Private-sector. 1,750+ corporate members including most of the Fortune 500. Requires a site visit. **Unaffected by the federal litigation.** |
| **State / city MBE** | State or municipal agency | Varies | Separate from NMSDC. Many public-works goals still apply. Some states have reciprocity. |
| **DBE** | State DOT via Unified Certification Program | $0 | 49 CFR Part 26. Personal net worth cap $2,047,000 effective May 9, 2024; retirement accounts and primary residence excluded. |
| **HUBZone** | SBA | $0 | Purely geographic — principal office in a HUBZone, 35% of employees residing in one. Carries a 10% price evaluation preference. Check the [SBA map](https://www.sba.gov/certifications/) first — this one is worth checking early because it's free and has nothing to do with the contested presumptions. |

**Recommended path: NMSDC MBE plus state/local MBE.** It targets corporate primes and municipal
goals without depending on contested federal presumptions. Check the HUBZone map for his address
since it costs nothing to look.

On the site, this is a badge row on the `/commercial` page and a line item on the capability
statement — see how [DECA](https://www.decaimprovements.com/) uses MBE certification as a procurement
asset rather than a values statement.

## Municipal and utility pipe work

### Licensing

Often a **separate license from general contracting**, and state-specific:

- **Florida** requires a Certified Underground Utility and Excavation Contractor license
  ([CILB 5-N](https://www.myfloridalicense.com/CheckListDetail.asp?XACT_DEFN_ID=18301&clientCode=0610&xactCode=1042))
  — four years of experience or education equivalent, a state exam, liability insurance, and workers'
  comp within 30 days.
- **Georgia** has Utility Manager and Utility Foreman licenses requiring 12 hours of approved safety
  training.

We need his state before we can say what applies. This may be a genuine opportunity: if he qualifies
for an underground utility license on experience, it's a credential very few multi-trade contractors
hold.

### Certifications that matter

Collect the cards for all of these that he has:

- **OSHA 30** (construction)
- **Competent person, trenching and excavation** — note that OSHA issues no card. Under 29 CFR 1926
  Subpart P and 1926.32(f) the *employer* designates a competent person, who must be present whenever
  workers are in an excavation. The deliverable is training plus a written designation letter.
- **Confined space entry**
- **Traffic control / flagger**
- **NPDES or state erosion control**
- **[NASSCO PACP/MACP/LACP](https://nassco.org/education-and-training/pacp-lacp-macp/)** — $1,270
  initial, valid 3 years, frequently *required* by municipalities for CCTV pipe inspection
  deliverables. If he doesn't have this and wants municipal work, it's a concrete, cheap thing to go
  get.
- **811 one-call** compliance — federal, non-negotiable
- Welding and backflow certifications, if held

### Getting onto bid lists

Register directly on city, county, utility, and water-district vendor portals. Watch state DOT
letting pages, school and special district procurement, and public plan rooms; aggregators like
BidNet and BidPrime cover the rest. Prequalification is agency-specific and usually precedes bidding.

SAM.gov is only required for *federal* contracts — but federally funded local projects carry federal
clauses, so registering is cheap insurance and it's how he gets the UEI and CAGE code for the
capability statement.

## Procurement vocabulary

Using these terms correctly signals "I've been through your process." Misusing them signals the
opposite, so confirm with Tino which he's actually worked under before publishing any of them.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Hard bid** | Lowest responsible bidder on complete drawings |
| **Design-Build** | One contract for both design and construction |
| **CMAR** | Construction Manager at Risk — CM joins pre-construction, then guarantees a maximum price |
| **JOC** | Job Order Contract — a standing contract drawn against by task order |
| **IDIQ** | Indefinite Delivery / Indefinite Quantity — similar standing-contract structure |
| **P3** | Public-Private Partnership |
| **Sourcewell** | Cooperative purchasing contract that lets agencies buy without their own bid |
| **Self-performance** | Percentage of work done by your own crews rather than subs |
| **EMR** | Experience Modification Rate — workers' comp loss-history multiplier; 1.0 is average |
| **COI** | Certificate of Insurance |
| **UEI / CAGE** | Federal entity identifiers from SAM.gov registration |

## What the `/commercial` page should contain

1. Hero that names the specialty concretely — pipe sizes, systems, agencies, years — not "commercial
   construction services"
2. Trust row: license classifications, insurance limits, bonding capacity, safety certifications
3. Core competencies, in agency language
4. Past performance — described in prose where photos are encumbered by prior employers (see
   [01-legal-and-licensing.md](01-legal-and-licensing.md#4-photos-the-prior-employer-problem-is-the-sharp-edge))
5. Self-performance statement and equipment list
6. Certifications and MBE badges
7. Named references with titles and organizations
8. **Download the capability statement** + a direct email for estimators
9. Explicitly: what he's looking for — subcontract scopes, job sizes, geography

Note the contrast with the homeowner page. Same person, same skills, almost no shared copy.
