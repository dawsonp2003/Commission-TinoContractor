# Reference Sites

21 live sites read directly, plus notes on where they're useful. Grouped by which of Tino's two
audiences they teach us about. Patterns extracted in [04-common-patterns.md](04-common-patterns.md);
the unusual stuff worth stealing is in [05-differentiators.md](05-differentiators.md).

Everything below was fetched and read unless marked otherwise. Interaction-dependent features
(drag sliders, animations) can't be verified by fetching, and are flagged where relevant.

---

## Best overall models for this project

**[davincidesignbuild.com](https://davincidesignbuild.com/)** — the strongest conversion design in
the set, and the closest thing to a template for Tino's homepage. The form is *in* the hero
("Takes 2 minutes — no site visit needed for a ballpark"), above a single-row trust strip:

> `5.0 Google · 30 Reviews` · `500+ Projects` · `Licensed, Bonded & Insured · WA #606007438` ·
> `Houzz Best of 2024` · `5-Year Warranty`

Note what that strip does: rating, volume, license number, third-party award, and warranty term in
one glance. The FAQ publishes real dollar ranges, and they use scarcity ("Only 3 project slots
remaining this month"). **Copy this trust strip almost literally.**

**[alphadevg.com/pricing](https://www.alphadevg.com/pricing/)** — the single most unusual page in the
study, and the biggest differentiator available to us. Three price tiers per service
(Kitchen $15–30K / $30–55K / $55–85K+), each with a timeline and a bill of scope, plus
Cost-vs-Value ROI percentages and a "Last updated: May 29, 2026" date. Their real differentiator is
a *policy*, not an adjective: they show clients actual material receipts instead of burying "a hidden
20–35% markup." A competitor can't copy that without changing how they operate — which is exactly
why it's credible. They also name the specific permit jurisdictions they pull in and state plainly
that skipping permits voids homeowner's insurance.

**[apexunderground.com](https://apexunderground.com/)** — Denver underground utility contractor, and
the closest *structural* analogue to Tino's dual identity. Three audience cards on entry —
**Municipal / Commercial / Residential** — each with its own scope list. Municipal gets emergency
rehab and mains, commercial gets vaults and fire lines, residential gets sewer and water-line
replacement. "Core Competencies" are framed as Project Controls, QA/QC, Safety Excellence, Advanced
Technology (HDD, pipebursting). Named projects include NIST Boulder and Denver Water's lead-line
program.

**[kegtus.com/what-we-do/utilities](https://www.kegtus.com/what-we-do/utilities/)** — the best B2B
content model found. A stats bar (50+ years, 400+ pieces of equipment, **75% self-performance**, 27+
active JOCs), fluent procurement vocabulary (CMAR, Design-Build, P3, JOC, IDIQ, Sourcewell, hard
bid), and testimonials attributed to **named public-agency staff with titles** ("Tom Cantrell, City
of Tucson Water"). One quote answers the fear a municipal buyer actually has: "they don't push for
change orders when it's not justified." Steal the vocabulary and the attribution style.

## Residential remodel / design-build

| Site | Worth noting |
| --- | --- |
| [potterconstruction.com](https://www.potterconstruction.com/) | Seattle, est. 1979. Portfolio tabbed by room (Baths / Kitchens / Whole House). Testimonials are full named paragraphs describing 10-month whole-house jobs — far more persuasive than one-line pull quotes. "Latest News" is mostly awards plus a charity ramp build. No license number found anywhere. |
| [jacksondesignandremodeling.com](https://www.jacksondesignandremodeling.com/) | San Diego. A "FEATURED IN" press wall (Architectural Digest, San Diego Magazine), **video** client success stories, 100+ third-party GuildQuality reviews, a physical showroom with hours, and a dated live event. Clients independently praise the project portal in their testimonials. |
| [letterfour.com](https://letterfour.com/) | LA design-build that argues a *thesis*: "when your architect and contractor aren't on the same page, you make expensive compromises." Footer carries both licenses (`Architecture #C30146 · Contractor #B1028949`). Has a referral program and an ADA accessibility contact. Also has a live broken link — its cost calculator 404s. |
| [rebath.com](https://www.rebath.com/) | Franchise, form-first, **no phone above the fold**. Heavy hidden UTM/GCLID/FBCLID attribution fields and an explicit SMS-consent checkbox — a good model for lead attribution if we ever run ads. |
| [kestingconstruction.com/financing](https://www.kestingconstruction.com/financing/) | An entire page on financing (Hearth, 17 lenders, soft credit pull) with its own 4-step process and FAQ. Relevant only if Tino wants to offer financing. |
| [dnaremodelingservices.com](https://dnaremodelingservices.com/hablamos-espanol/) | Free 3D renderings before commitment, an online client project portal with real-time updates, and a single dedicated Spanish landing page. |

## Multi-trade / handyman / owner-operator — closest to Tino's scale

| Site | Worth noting |
| --- | --- |
| [acehandymanservices.com](https://www.acehandymanservices.com/) | **Productized labor**: half-day (4hr) and full-day (8hr) packages. Removes the "will he even quote a small job?" hesitation entirely. 1-year workmanship guarantee, zip-code-first entry, and a pointed jab at the category — "Multi-Skilled Handymen who are **employees, not sub-contractors**." |
| [decaimprovements.com](https://www.decaimprovements.com/) | Latino-owned, Champaign IL. **The best model for the bilingual and heritage angle.** Nav grouped by *outcome* (Roofing & Exterior / Remodeling & Construction / Property Improvement) rather than a flat 15-item trade list. Three hero trust hexagons: Lifetime Warranty, **Bilingual Service**, Free Estimates, with a documented 24–48 hour response time. Its About page insists bilingual service is "built into the company's core service model, not offered as an occasional accommodation." Also carries **MBE certification** — the only site in the set that converts heritage into a procurement asset. |
| [ibarraremodelingtx.com](https://ibarraremodelingtx.com/) | Midland TX. "Fully Insured & Licensed · Free Estimates & Bilingual Attention" in the hero. Lists payment methods (Zelle, Cash App) and service radius ("We Cover 300 Miles"). Its weakness: "Residential and Commercial" as one undifferentiated heading, which serves neither audience. |
| [amjies.com](https://amjies.com/) | Hispanic-owned Chicagoland roofer. Owner named with her own cell (Angie Perez), a **WhatsApp** link, separate `Español` and `Contáctenos` nav items, and an insurance-claim-number field in the contact form. For a one-person operation, the personal cell is a feature. |
| [jandkcontractingofiowa.com](https://jandkcontractingofiowa.com/) | Plain, fast, effective. Sells the *bundling* argument well: "you don't have to worry about coordinating between extra companies" — precisely Tino's pitch as a multi-trade contractor. |

## Civil / underground utility

Covered above: [apexunderground.com](https://apexunderground.com/),
[kegtus.com](https://www.kegtus.com/what-we-do/utilities/),
[jandkcontractingofiowa.com](https://jandkcontractingofiowa.com/). Plus:

**[valverdeconst.com](https://valverdeconst.com/)** — genuinely useful and genuinely cautionary. The
good: the FAQ publishes the full license with classifications (`#276469, classifications A, B, C10,
C16, C36`), DVBE certification, named municipal contracts (City of LA, LA County, Corona, Signal
Hill), a 24-hour emergency line *separate* from the main line, and a note that they self-perform
traffic control rather than subbing it. The bad is in the next doc — it's a strong company with a
site actively undercutting it.

## Sites cited by others but not directly verified

Included because the patterns are worth knowing; treat the claims as secondhand.

- **MAREK** — navigation split into **Scopes / Services / Sectors**, with projects filterable by
  sector, scope, *and* location ([Atomicdust case study](https://www.atomicdust.com/marek-construction-new-website/)).
  The three-axis filter is a strong idea for a contractor with many trades.
- **Michilli** — a dedicated **subcontractor portal** with prominent "Get Paid" and "Become a
  Partner" actions ([e9digital](https://e9digital.com/building-a-timeless-nyc-website-for-an-upscale-general-contractor/)).
  Only relevant much later, but note that a real operational tool became the site's selling point.
- **Re-Bath / Bath Fitter** — before/after slider in the hero of every service page; an interactive
  bathroom designer ([PipelineOn](https://pipelineon.com/blog/best-remodeling-websites/)).
- **Rivera-Ulloa** (San Antonio) — two phone numbers, one labeled English and one Spanish. The site
  returned a 500 error when fetched; the detail comes from the search index. Their About copy also
  links to a *different company's* testimonials page, which suggests a templated build.

## One aesthetic to deliberately avoid

The [Awwwards construction category](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/construction/) optimizes for
WebGL, parallax, and scroll-jacking. That vocabulary sells agencies to enterprise clients; it
actively hurts a contractor whose buyer is on a phone with one bar of signal comparing three bids.

**Every genuinely effective site in this study was fast, plain, and dense with specifics.** That's
the target.
