# Lead Generation, Conversion & Local SEO

The uncomfortable framing up front: **the website is probably not the most valuable asset in this
project — the Google Business Profile is.** Practitioner surveys put GBP signals at roughly a third
of local-pack ranking weight. The site's main SEO job is to support the profile, and its main
conversion job is to get a phone call or text into Tino's hand fast.

---

## What homeowners actually weigh

Houzz asked homeowners for their top three hiring factors
([pro.houzz.com](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-learn/blog/what-homeowners-want-from-pros-right-now)):

| Factor | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Recommendations / references | 64% |
| Experience with similar project **scope** | 47% |
| Pricing that fits budget | 39% |
| Being licensed | 26% |
| Availability | 17% |
| Personality | 17% |
| Experience with similar **style** | 16% |

**The scope-vs-style gap (47% vs 16%) is the most actionable number here.** Organize the portfolio by
*type and size of job*, not by aesthetic. "Whole-house addition, 1,400 sq ft, 7 months" does more
work than "modern farmhouse."

A separate Houzz survey (July 2024, n=2,866) found the biggest struggles were finding someone
trustworthy (48%), getting clear detailed proposals (34%), and timely accurate communication (30%).
The [2025 Houzz & Home Study](https://st.hzcdn.com/static/econ/2025-US-Houzz-and-Home--Study.pdf)
reports 9 in 10 renovating homeowners hired a pro, with "finding the right service providers" the top
challenge at 33%.

Modernize's [2025 Homeowner Insights Report](https://modernize.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Homeowner-Insights-Report-2025.pdf)
inverts it usefully — what makes homeowners *disengage*: pushy sales tactics (37.9%), unclear quotes
(32.6%), lack of communication (25.5%).

> **Implication:** the highest-leverage page may not be "About" — it's a clear, itemized explanation
> of how Tino estimates and communicates. Publishing a sample (redacted) estimate would directly
> answer the top three complaints in both surveys.

## Reviews

BrightLocal's [Local Consumer Review Survey 2025](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)
(n=1,026 US adults):

- Trust in reviews as much as personal recommendations has **fallen to 42%**, down from 79% in 2020
- **83%** read reviews on Google; 44% Yelp; 40% Facebook
- **89%** expect owners to respond to reviews — positive ones too
- **74%** check at least two review sites before deciding

So Google reviews are necessary but no longer sufficient. Two implications for the plan: get him onto
a second platform (Facebook is the cheapest), and make responding to every review part of his
routine. Recency beats raw volume, so a steady trickle beats a launch-day burst — which also looks
less manufactured.

## Speed to lead

The famous statistic — "5-minute responders are 21x more likely to qualify a lead" — is worth
handling carefully, because it's everywhere and it's weaker than it looks.

It comes from a **2007 conference deck** by Dr. James Oldroyd (MIT Sloan) with InsideSales.com,
presented at MarketingSherpa on October 16, 2007
([PDF](https://www.onecavo.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/MIT-InsideSales.com_Lead-Response-Management.pdf)).
Its actual claims: odds of *contacting* a lead drop 100x from 5 to 30 minutes; odds of *qualifying*
drop 21x. But it covered six companies all using the vendor's own platform, measured outbound phone
dials against web-form leads, and states plainly that it "did not address close ratios." It is not a
conversion rate and not a revenue figure. Don't put it in a client deck as fact.

The better-sourced companion is the 2011 *Harvard Business Review* audit of 2,241 US companies:
average response time 42 hours, **23% never responded at all**, and firms contacting within an hour
were ~7x more likely to qualify than those waiting one more hour.

**Practical read:** the exact multiplier is unknowable for our context, but intent decays fast and
most competitors are genuinely slow. That's the opportunity. Design for a sub-five-minute
*acknowledgment*, which is achievable even from a roof:

- Click-to-call as the primary action, in a sticky mobile bar
- An SMS option next to it
- An instant auto-text on form submit confirming receipt and giving a realistic callback window
- A stated response-time promise only if he can keep it

## Form design

Baymard Institute's 2024 research is the best-sourced anchor and it contradicts the popular advice:
**"the number of form fields in a checkout impacts overall usability far more than the number of
steps."**

I'd flag that the widely circulated "multi-step forms convert 86% higher" and "13.85% vs 4.53%"
figures should not be used. [Digital Applied traced them](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/multi-step-quote-flow-design-conversion-2026)
and found no disclosed sample, industry mix, or methodology, and the HubSpot attribution appears to
be fabricated.

Defensible recommendations:

- **Cut fields before adding steps.** Start with name, phone, service type. That's it.
- **Bank the lead in step 1.** Contact info first, qualifying detail after — then abandonment costs
  us qualification, not the lead.
- **Photo upload is worth the friction here.** It lets Tino pre-qualify and quote accurately without
  a site visit, which is exactly the "unclear quotes" objection. Da Vinci's "no site visit needed for
  a ballpark" is the pitch.
- Budget range and timeline pickers go *after* the commit, and stay optional.
- Ordering matters more than count: open with an easy, low-commitment question (service-type tiles),
  not "what's your phone number?"

## Google Business Profile

The [Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey](https://www.garretthandley.com/google-business-profile-optimization-for-contractors-the-2026-complete-guide)
(47 practitioners rating 187 signals) allocates roughly: GBP signals 32%, reviews ~20%, on-page 15%,
then links, behavioral, citations. Expert opinion rather than Google data — directional only, but
consistently directional.

**Primary category is the strongest single relevance lever.** It determines which queries the profile
is *eligible* for at all. The common mistake is aspirational: choosing "General Contractor" when the
revenue is in kitchen remodels. Since Tino spans many trades, pick the primary by where the money
actually is and add secondaries for the rest. This is why
"[which two or three services do you most want to be hired for?](02-needed-from-tino.md#the-revenue-question-drives-seo-and-the-whole-homepage)"
is on the intake list.

Operational floor from practitioner guidance: 10+ real project photos with monthly additions, weekly
"What's New" posts, and a steady review cadence.

Also worth setting up: **Google Local Services Ads**, which carries the "Google Screened" badge and
requires license and insurance verification — a credential Tino will have and many competitors won't.

## Service pages vs. city pages — a real risk

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) name
this explicitly under *Doorway abuse*:

> "Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to
> one page" … "Blocks of text that list cities and regions that a web page is trying to rank for"

John Mueller has warned specifically against building out city-name landing page sets, and the March
2024 scaled content abuse policy extended this to AI-generated page sets. Many contractor marketing
agencies still sell exactly this.

**Recommendation:** build deep service pages first. Add area pages only where we have something real
— local project photos, a named local testimonial, specifics about that jurisdiction's permit office
or soil and climate conditions. A handful of genuine ones, never template-and-swap.

## Structured data — three things changed recently

- **Use `GeneralContractor`** (a subtype of `HomeAndConstructionBusiness` → `LocalBusiness`) for the
  business, plus `Service` on service pages.
- **Do not put `aggregateRating` or `review` on his own site.** Google's
  [review snippet documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)
  states: *"If the entity that's being reviewed controls the reviews about itself, their pages that
  use `LocalBusiness` or any other type of `Organization` structured data are ineligible for star
  review feature."* Display reviews visually; just don't mark them up.
- **`FAQPage` rich results are dead.** Restricted to government and health sites in August 2023, then
  [deprecated entirely on May 7, 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes),
  with Search Console reporting removed in June 2026. `HowTo` is gone too. Write FAQ content anyway —
  it still feeds AI Overviews and featured snippets — but the markup buys nothing.
- A guideline added **July 24, 2026** bans fake or undisclosed incentivized reviews on-page and in
  markup, with manual action as the penalty.

## Performance

Portfolio sites die on image weight, and Tino's whole value proposition is photos. Non-negotiables:

- AVIF/WebP with responsive `srcset`
- Lazy-loading below the fold, eager on the hero image
- Explicit `width`/`height` on every image to prevent layout shift
- A CDN, and images served from an image CDN rather than our own origin (see
  [08-tech-stack-and-hosting.md](08-tech-stack-and-hosting.md#so-why-still-use-an-image-cdn))
- Test on a throttled mobile connection, not a desktop

Trend pieces are calling for mobile LCP under 1.5s and images under 100KB. Whether or not those exact
thresholds hold, a static site with a proper image pipeline clears them comfortably, which is a
reason to prefer that architecture.

## Lead sources beyond the site

**Residential.** Google Local Services Ads, Angi, Thumbtack, Houzz, Nextdoor, Yelp, Porch. Most
charge per lead and resell the same lead to competitors — which is precisely why speed-to-lead
matters more there than anywhere. Worth listing on a couple, not all.

**Commercial.** Primes run their own portals (Procore, Buildertrend, BuildingConnected). Registration
is per-GC.

**Public work.** City, county, utility, and water-district vendor portals, registered directly. State
DOT letting pages, school and special district procurement, public plan rooms, and aggregators
(BidNet, BidPrime). SAM.gov is only required for *federal* contracts, though federally funded local
projects carry federal clauses. Details in
[07-commercial-bid-playbook.md](07-commercial-bid-playbook.md).

Word of mouth is almost certainly his biggest channel already (64% of homeowners cite
recommendations). The site's job for that traffic is different: someone who was *referred* to Tino
arrives already sold and just needs confirmation he's real, licensed, and reachable. That audience is
served by the trust strip and the phone number, not by SEO.
