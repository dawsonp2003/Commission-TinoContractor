## 1. The important legal stuff (please do this part first)

We're not being nosy — some states fine you for getting this wrong on a website, and one state makes
it a misdemeanor. So we want to get it right.

- What **state and city** do you work out of?
- What's your **contractor license number**, and what does the license actually say? If you can send
  a **photo of the license itself**, that's easiest. We need the classification codes (things like
  "B" or "C-10") because they tell us what we're allowed to advertise.
- Which board or agency issued it, and when does it expire?
- **When you say "certified," what does that mean exactly?** Which certification, from who? It's a
  specific legal word in some states so we want to use it correctly.

### The big one: electrical and plumbing

We know you can do this work. The question is different — **do you hold a separate electrical or
plumbing license?**

In most states those are licensed separately from general contracting, and advertising them without
the license can be a misdemeanor even if you're perfectly capable. So:

- Do you have an electrical license? A plumbing license?
- If not, are you fine with the site saying you *coordinate licensed electrical and plumbing subs* as
  part of bigger jobs? (That's true, it's legal everywhere, and it still tells people you handle the
  whole project.)

Just tell us honestly — it changes how we word things, not whether we can build the site.

### Business setup

- Is the **LLC formed** yet? What's the exact name on it?
- Does that name match your license exactly? (Even small differences matter.)
- Do you have an EIN?
- **Insurance:** do you carry general liability? What are the limits? Workers' comp, or are you exempt
  as the owner? Commercial auto? Who's your agent?
- Are you **bonded**? By who, for how much?

## 2. Name, contact, and how people reach you

- **What do you want the business called?** You mentioned you had an idea — what is it? Send a couple
  of options if you have them and we'll check what's available as a domain before anything's locked in.
- Do you have a **logo**? If not, no problem, we'll plan for one.

### How people reach you — please be honest here

This is more important than it sounds. We're going to design the whole site around it, and the #1
reason people hire one contractor over another is that he picked up the phone first. So we'd rather
build around what's actually true than what sounds good:

- **Best phone number.** Business line, or your cell?
- **When someone calls during the workday, can you realistically pick up?** If you're on a roof, you
  can't — that's fine, we just need to know.
- **Do you text?** A lot of people would rather text than call.
- **Do you use WhatsApp?** (Worth asking specifically — almost no contractor sites have it and it's
  really common with Spanish-speaking customers.)
- If someone messages you at 7pm on a Saturday, what happens?
- **How fast can you get back to somebody, honestly?** Two hours? Same day? Next morning? We'd like
  to put a promise on the site, but only one you can actually keep.
- Best email for you?

### Where you work

- How far will you drive?
- Which **cities and neighborhoods** do you actually want to work in? Name them — it genuinely helps
  people find you in search.
- Do you want your **address on the website**, or just the area you cover? (If you work out of your
  house, you don't have to publish it.)
- Normal working hours? Do you do emergency calls?

### Anything you're already on

Do you have a Google Business listing, Facebook, Instagram, Angi, Yelp, Thumbtack, or Houzz page? Send
links. We'd rather take over what exists than accidentally create a duplicate, which hurts you in
search.

## 3. The work you want

- **What two or three kinds of jobs do you most want the phone to ring about?** Not everything you
  *can* do — what you actually want more of. This one answer affects how well you show up in Google
  more than anything else on this list.
- Is there work you'd rather not do anymore?
- What's the **smallest job** worth your time? Would you take a $400 drywall repair, or is that a
  distraction?
- What's the biggest job you'd take on alone?
- Do you plan on staying solo, or hiring a crew eventually?

### Pricing (optional, but this could be your biggest advantage)

We looked at 21 contractor websites. Only **two** posted any prices. The ones that do get better
leads, because people who can't afford it don't call, and people who can already know they can.

If you're up for it, for each of your main services: a **rough range** (like "$8K–15K for a typical
bathroom"), roughly **how long** it takes, and what's **included**. Even "starts around $X" helps.

Totally your call — if you'd rather talk price in person, we'll leave it off.

## 4. Your projects

This is the part that actually sells the work. **Aim for 8–12 projects.** Eight good ones beat thirty
thin ones.

**Start with your own place** — the addition where you doubled the square footage, the 4-car carport,
the outdoor kitchen with the roof and sink and stove, and the renovations. Those are perfect, because
it's your own property, so there's nobody to ask permission from.

There's a template at [`project-template.md`](project-template.md), but honestly a voice memo per
project works fine. For each one we want to know:

- What was it, and what did you actually do? (List the trades — "framing, roofing, electrical rough-in,
  drywall, tile")
- How big? Square footage, number of rooms, whatever fits
- How long did it take?
- What city, what year?
- Roughly what did it cost, or a range? (Optional)
- **Was there a problem you had to solve?** Something unexpected, something tricky, something another
  guy would've done wrong? *This is the most interesting part and the part everybody leaves out.*
- Did you pull permits? Which city?
- Would the customer be willing to be a reference or leave a review?

### One important thing about photos

For each set of photos, we need to know **three things**:

1. Did **you** take the photos?
2. Whose property is it — yours, or a customer's?
3. **Was this work you did for a company you worked for?**

That last one matters. Photos from jobs you did while employed somewhere may legally belong to that
employer, and municipal and commercial work is often under a confidentiality agreement. We don't want
to cause you a problem, so if it's employer work we'll **describe it in words instead** — which
honestly works fine for the big pipe jobs anyway.

For customer jobs, we'll need a quick signed OK from them. We can give you a simple form.

## 5. Photos

**20–40 photos minimum.** Real ones, from your phone. Never stock photos — more than half of
contractor websites use fake photos and people can tell instantly.

Full guide with tips: [`photo-guide.md`](photo-guide.md). Short version:

- **Before AND after, from the same spot.** This is the single most valuable thing you can send. We
  can build a slider people drag back and forth. Almost no contractor at your level has this.
- **Photos of you working.** Face visible, on a real jobsite. Only about 4 out of the 21 sites we
  looked at had real photos of the actual person. People are hiring *you*.
- One decent photo of you for the About page
- **Close-up detail shots** — tile edges, trim, a clean panel, finished corners. These prove your
  finish quality way better than wide shots.
- Any video at all, even just walking through a finished room while talking. Almost nobody does this.

## 6. Your story

The About page is usually the second-most-visited page on a contractor site, after the homepage.
**Voice memo is ideal here.** Just talk:

- How'd you get into this? How long have you been doing it?
- Tell us about the big construction company work — the sewer and drainage systems, the pipe
  networks. What was your actual role? Were you designing them, building them, running crews?
- **Why are you going independent now?** People genuinely want to know.
- What do you care about in the work? What do you see other guys do that bothers you?
- What are you proudest of?
- What do customers say about you? What do they seem surprised by?

Also:

- Total years of experience?
- Do you offer a **warranty**? How long, covering what? (Only 7 of 21 sites say — it stands out.)
- **How do you handle estimates and communication?** Walk us through it. Turns out the biggest
  complaints homeowners have are unclear quotes and bad communication, so if you're good at this,
  it's worth a whole page. If you've got an old estimate you could send with the customer's info
  blacked out, that'd be great.
- Do you pull permits yourself? Which cities do you deal with?
- Any reviews or testimonials you already have?
- Anybody who'd vouch for you — old bosses, customers, other contractors?

### Spanish

- Do you want the site in **Spanish as well as English**?
- Do you prefer speaking Spanish with customers?
- Should there be a separate Spanish phone line, or is it the same number?

We'd suggest doing the Spanish, honestly. It's useful for customers, and on the commercial side it's
a real selling point to a general contractor who needs someone who can run a Spanish-speaking crew.

## 7. The commercial and city/county work

This part is about getting hired by **companies and cities** rather than homeowners. Your pipe network
background is the rarest thing about you, and it's worth a lot more money than residential. Take your
time on this one — it can come after the rest.

### The pipe work, in detail

- Which companies did you work for, and what years?
- What kinds of systems? Sanitary sewer, storm drainage, water mains — all of it?
- **How big?** Pipe diameters, materials, depths, lengths, project dollar values.
- Which **cities, counties, or agencies** was the work for? Can we name them?
- **What was your actual role?** Laborer, operator, foreman, superintendent, designer? Be specific —
  "designing" pipe networks is a big claim and we want to describe it accurately.
- Anything you did that was unusually hard or unusual?

### Certifications — send photos of any cards you have

- OSHA 10 or OSHA 30?
- Competent person for trenching/excavation?
- Confined space entry?
- Traffic control or flagger?
- NASSCO PACP/MACP/LACP (the pipe camera inspection one)? — *cities often require this, so it's worth
  getting if you don't have it*
- Welding? Backflow? Erosion control?
- Anything else, even expired

### Equipment and capacity

- What equipment do you **own**? Excavator, skid steer, trailer, compactor, laser level, pipe laser?
- What can you rent or get access to?
- What do you do yourself vs. sub out? (Companies like to see a number here, like "I self-perform 80%")
- Do you have a **bonding** relationship with a surety company? Any capacity letter?
- Do you keep books? QuickBooks or similar? Do you have a CPA? (Bigger jobs require financial
  statements — smaller ones usually don't, so this isn't urgent.)
- References from the commercial side — old supervisors, city contacts, general contractors?

### One heads-up on minority business certification

You may have heard that being Hispanic gets you automatic access to set-aside government contracts.
**That changed.** Federal courts struck down the automatic racial presumption in 2023, and the SBA
formally removed it effective September 2026. Same for the DOT's DBE program.

There are still real options — private-sector MBE certification through NMSDC, state and city MBE
programs, and HUBZone if your address qualifies (that one's free to check and has nothing to do with
ethnicity). We'll look into it properly. Just wanted you to hear it from us rather than from someone
selling outdated advice.

Questions for you: do you own 51%+ of the business? Are you interested in pursuing certification?
